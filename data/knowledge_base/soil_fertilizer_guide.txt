SOIL HEALTH AND FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT GUIDE

UNDERSTANDING SOIL HEALTH
Healthy soil is the foundation of productive farming. Key parameters:

1. SOIL pH (Reaction)
- pH 5.5–6.5: Slightly acidic — best for rice, potato, groundnut, banana
- pH 6.0–7.0: Slightly acidic to neutral — best for maize, wheat, soybean, cotton
- pH 6.5–7.5: Neutral — best for tomato, onion, garlic, most vegetables
- pH 7.5–8.5: Slightly alkaline — barley, sugarcane, mustard can tolerate
- Correction:
  * Acidic soils: Apply agricultural lime (CaCO₃) @ 1–3 t/ha. Re-test after 2 seasons.
  * Alkaline soils: Apply gypsum (CaSO₄) @ 2–4 t/ha. Leach with good quality water.

2. ORGANIC CARBON (OC)
- Low: < 0.5% — Needs immediate organic matter addition
- Medium: 0.5–0.75% — Add compost and green manure
- High: > 0.75% — Maintain with minimal disturbance
- Improvement: Apply FYM 10–15 t/ha, vermicompost 2–3 t/ha, crop residue incorporation

3. NITROGEN (N)
- Available N: Low < 280 kg/ha; Medium 280–560 kg/ha; High > 560 kg/ha
- Nitrogen sources: Urea (46% N), DAP (18% N), Ammonium Sulphate (21% N)
- Always split N application (3 doses) to reduce losses from leaching and volatilisation

4. PHOSPHORUS (P₂O₅)
- Available P: Low < 11 kg/ha; Medium 11–22 kg/ha; High > 22 kg/ha
- Sources: DAP (46% P₂O₅), SSP (16% P₂O₅), Rock Phosphate (18–20%)
- Apply at sowing — phosphorus does not move in soil

5. POTASSIUM (K₂O)
- Available K: Low < 110 kg/ha; Medium 110–280 kg/ha; High > 280 kg/ha
- Sources: MOP/Muriate of Potash (60% K₂O), SOP/Sulphate of Potash (50% K₂O)
- Critical for drought resistance, fruit quality, and disease resistance

6. MICRONUTRIENTS
- Zinc (Zn): Most common deficiency in India (70% soils deficient).
  Apply ZnSO₄ @ 25 kg/ha at sowing or foliar spray 0.5% ZnSO₄.
- Boron (B): Essential for cotton, mustard, groundnut.
  Apply Borax @ 10 kg/ha or foliar spray 0.2% Boric acid.
- Iron (Fe): Common in alkaline soils — foliar spray FeSO₄ 1% + Citric acid 0.5%
- Sulphur (S): Deficiency in mustard, onion, garlic. Apply Gypsum 100 kg/ha.

FERTILIZER APPLICATION METHODS
- Broadcasting: Simple, but 20–30% wastage. Use for P and K at land preparation.
- Band placement: Place fertilizer 5 cm from seed. More efficient for P.
- Fertigation: Apply through drip irrigation — increases efficiency by 30–40%.
- Foliar spray: Quick correction of micronutrient deficiency. Maximum uptake at leaf.

ORGANIC FARMING TECHNIQUES
- Biofertilizers: Rhizobium (legumes), Azotobacter (non-legumes), PSB, Blue-Green Algae
- Compost preparation: Layer green matter (N), dry matter (C), soil, in 6:3:1 ratio.
  Water to maintain moisture 50–60%. Turn every 2 weeks. Ready in 3 months.
- Vermicomposting: Eisenia fetida earthworms process organic waste.
  Ratio: food waste : bedding = 1:3. Ready in 45–60 days.
- Green Manuring: Dhaincha (Sesbania), Sunhemp, Cowpea — incorporate at flowering stage.
  Adds 60–80 kg N/ha equivalent.

SOIL CONSERVATION
- Contour farming reduces erosion by 50–80% on slopes.
- Cover crops (Cowpea, Clover) prevent erosion during fallow period.
- Mulching with crop residue conserves moisture and reduces weed growth.
- Minimum tillage preserves soil structure and beneficial microorganisms.

SOIL TESTING — HOW TO COLLECT SAMPLES
1. Divide your field into uniform blocks of 1–2 ha each.
2. Take 10–15 sub-samples from 0–15 cm depth in a zigzag pattern.
3. Mix sub-samples thoroughly; take 500 g composite sample.
4. Fill in sample information card and submit to nearest Soil Testing Laboratory.
5. Receive Soil Health Card within 30 days with specific fertilizer recommendations.

WATER AND IRRIGATION MANAGEMENT
- Flood irrigation efficiency: 35–45%
- Sprinkler irrigation efficiency: 65–75%
- Drip irrigation efficiency: 85–95%
- Water requirement varies: Rice (1,200–2,500 mm), Wheat (350–600 mm), 
  Cotton (700–1,200 mm), Vegetables (300–700 mm)
- Schedule irrigation based on soil moisture (tensiometer readings) not calendar.
