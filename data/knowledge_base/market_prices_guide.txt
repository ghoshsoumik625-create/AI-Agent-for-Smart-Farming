MARKET PRICES AND AGRI-ECONOMICS GUIDE

UNDERSTANDING MANDI PRICES
India has a regulated agricultural marketing system. Key terms:
- Minimum Support Price (MSP): Floor price guaranteed by government for select crops.
- Modal Price: Most common transaction price in a mandi on a given day.
- Mandi Arrival: Quantity of produce arriving at a particular market.
- Floor Price vs. Ceiling Price: Range within which commodity prices fluctuate.

HOW TO SELL PRODUCE FOR BEST PRICE

1. THROUGH MANDI/APMC
- Bring graded, dried produce (moisture within norms).
- Arrive early morning for best auction prices.
- Bring identity proof (Aadhaar) and land documents.
- Obtain Market Arrival Receipt after sale.
- Payment within 3 days is mandatory under APMC rules.

2. THROUGH ePM-FPO / eNAM (National Agriculture Market)
- Register at enam.gov.in with farm details.
- Upload produce quality photos.
- Receive bids from buyers across India.
- Better price discovery, transparent system.
- 7,000+ mandis on eNAM platform.

3. THROUGH FARMER PRODUCER ORGANIZATIONS (FPOs)
- Pool produce with other farmers for collective bargaining.
- Direct sales to food processing companies at 15–25% premium.
- Reduce transport costs through bulk selling.
- Contact NABARD for nearest FPO registration.

4. DIRECT MARKETING OPTIONS
- Rythu Bazaars (Andhra Pradesh, Telangana): Direct farmer to consumer.
- Harit (Green) Mandis (Maharashtra): Weekly vegetable markets.
- Apni Mandi (Punjab, Haryana): Weekend farmers' markets.
- Online platforms: Agribazaar.com, Dehaat, AgroStar, BigHaat.

PRICE INFLUENCING FACTORS
- Monsoon performance: 70% of Indian farming is rain-fed. Drought = price spike.
- MSP announcement: Government purchase operations stabilise floor prices.
- International commodity prices: Soybean, cotton, wheat prices linked to global markets.
- Export-import policy: Export bans on onion, wheat can crash domestic prices.
- Cold storage capacity: States with good cold chain infrastructure have stable vegetable prices.
- Harvest season: Prices typically drop 20–40% during peak harvest; recover in off-season.

AGRICULTURAL LOANS AND FINANCE
- Kisan Credit Card (KCC): Short-term crop loans up to ₹3 lakh at 7% (4% with subsidy).
  Repayment: within crop season. No margin for loans up to ₹1.6 lakh.
- NABARD Crop Loans: State cooperative banks. Interest subvention 2%.
- PM-KISAN: ₹6,000/year direct income support. Register at pmkisan.gov.in.
- Agricultural Term Loans: For land development, irrigation, machinery — 5–7% interest.

CROP INSURANCE
PMFBY (Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana):
- Premium: 2% for kharif; 1.5% for rabi; 5% for commercial/horticulture.
- Government pays remaining premium (up to 90%) to insurance companies.
- Covers: Drought, flood, hailstorm, cyclone, post-harvest losses.
- Enrol through bank at time of KCC loan or separately before cut-off date.
- Claim settlement: Within 2 months of harvest/damage assessment.
